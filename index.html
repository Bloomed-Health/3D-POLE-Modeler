Function · in vivo

Pol ε performs leading-strand synthesis at the eukaryotic replisome, copying the template 3′→5′ while extending the nascent strand 5′→3′ at >50 nt/s. Base-substitution error rate approaches 10−7 per nucleotide incorporated.

Proofreading mechanism Active dispute · 2025–2026

The intrinsic 3′→5′ exonuclease excises misincorporated nucleotides via an intramolecular shuttling mechanism, switching the primer terminus between the polymerase and exonuclease active sites separated by ~40 Å (measured from Roske & Yeeles 2024 cryo-EM structures; not computed from this schematic model’s coordinates). The mismatch-recognition pathway proceeds through four structurally resolved states: post-insertion → arrest → frayed substrate → mismatch excision.

Structures9F6I · 9F6J · 9F6K · 9F6L SourceRoske & Yeeles, NSMB 2024 ↗

An alternative pathway proposes that the primer terminus can be transferred to the exonuclease site through a different conformational route, potentially involving partial strand separation rather than full intramolecular threading. The relative contributions of these two pathways remain an active area of investigation.

Catalytic mechanism

Two-metal-ion catalysis: Metal A (Mg²⁺) lowers the pKa of the primer 3′-OH, generating the nucleophile. Metal B (Mg²⁺) coordinates the β,γ-phosphates of the incoming dNTP and stabilizes the pentacoordinate transition state. Both metals are chelated by conserved aspartate carboxylates D626 (motif A) and D860/D862 (motif C) (He et al. 2024, PDB 9B8S).
